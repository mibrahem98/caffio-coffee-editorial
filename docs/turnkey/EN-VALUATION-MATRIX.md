## 1. Valuation basis

The entity card identifies Caffio as a private digital asset with no independently auditable revenue, EBITDA, SDE, traffic, retention, or customer dataset. Revenue, EBITDA, SDE, and DCF methods are therefore **not applicable**: using them would turn unavailable assumptions into financial facts. [1]

This matrix instead distinguishes the **non-exclusive template-license floor**, the context of **operating-business listings**, and a transparent marketing band for the current asset package. It does not collapse those categories into one misleading “comparable.”

| Method | Use case | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue / EBITDA / SDE multiples | Requires verified operating data | **Unavailable / not applicable** |
| Precedent acquisition transactions | Requires closed transactions with similar rights, support, and operating scope | **No direct comparable transaction found in this research** |
| Template licence prices | Establishes a non-exclusive commodity floor | **Usable as a floor context only** |
| Operating-business listings | Demonstrates how supplier, brand, and revenue claims affect asks | **Context only; not a multiple to apply** |
| Prepared digital-asset package | Separates code, documentation, rights scope, and support | **Selected framing** |

## 3. Analytical marketing bands for the current asset

The bands below are **not observed comparable sales or closing prices**. They are analytical marketing judgments. Their logic is that Caffio is above a commodity template licence because it contains bilingual UX, SSR metadata, tests, moderation controls, and handover documentation; it remains below an operating business because there is no evidenced revenue, transferrable customer base, live provider account, supplier, inventory, or fulfilment operation.

| Delivery tier | Included scope | Analytical marketing band (USD) | Rationale | Non-negotiable conditions |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **As-Is: source + documentation, no live operation** | Codebase, current documents, stated validation results, and disclosed simulations; no implied transfer of provider accounts, registered marks, or ongoing support | **2,500–5,000** | More developed than a commodity licence, while preserving buyer execution risk for production content and operations | Clearly state: no proven revenue, no live commerce, and no automatic transfer of third-party assets. |
| **Controlled transfer package** | The above plus an auditable asset/rights inventory, defined technical handover session, and written technical acceptance | **5,000–9,000** | Delivery risk falls when asset rights, acceptance, and finite support scope are documented | Requires a counsel-reviewed transfer agreement, an asset list, and an explicit schedule of support hours/scope. |
| **Potential value after evidenced activation** | The package plus auditable product/batch records, tested payment/shipping/email integrations, operating evidence, and legal proof | **Reassess; no pre-set figure** | The nature of the asset can change only once operational and financial evidence exists | Do not price it as an operating business before clean, reviewable operating data exists for a meaningful comparison period. |

### How to use the bands

Do not present a band as “true value” or as a promised outcome. A seller may start negotiations inside the tier that actually matches the agreed transfer scope, rights inventory, and support package; final price remains subject to buyer diligence, ownership confirmation, and contract terms. The As-Is tier excludes registered trademarks, vendor accounts, paid subscriptions, third-party photo/font licences, and ongoing service obligations unless a written agreement expressly includes them.

## 4. Digital-asset handover matrix

| Item | Evidenced current status | Pricing effect | Buyer/seller action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bilingual UX and RTL/LTR | Present in the application and handover material | Supports As-Is differentiation | Re-test copy, language, and direction after rebranding. |
| Search, comparison, and SSR | Present in the documented implementation and test approach | Supports technical differentiation | Re-test against buyer environment and buyer records. |
| Reflection governance | Pending/approved/rejected model; no seeded public feedback | Reduces fabricated-content risk | Set review policy and accountable operator. |
| Payment, shipping, and email | Clearly disclosed local simulations only | Prevents an operating-store price claim | Choose, integrate, and test actual providers. |
| Product and batch evidence | Unsupported facts remain pending; verified tasting data is deferred | Lowers live-operational readiness, not source-code utility | Collect and enter auditable product/batch source records. |
| Rights, accounts, and assets | No automatic transfer | Material negotiation factor | Inventory ownership/licensing and use a separate written agreement. |
| Post-sale support | Not implied | Separates As-Is from controlled transfer | Define hours, scope, exclusions, and price in writing. |
